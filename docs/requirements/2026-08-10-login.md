# The login screen

## Who this is for

The owner's words, 2026-08-10:

> "This is for every user that wants to login to the App and fully access the features available."

**Everyone who opens Dapur Naura.** The screen is the first thing the app shows, so there is no
visitor who does not meet it and no part of the app reachable without passing through it.

## What they need

**A login screen, drawn as the supplied design, that lets anyone through without checking anything.**

The owner's instruction, given in English on 2026-08-10:

> "Let's create another screen which is Login. Can you translate an image into User Requirement?"

Answering the agent's question on whether this is real authentication or a screen:

> "A screen, no API call. Here's the UI Design from My Figma."

**This is the appearance of logging in and none of the machinery.** Nothing is sent anywhere,
nothing is verified, nothing is remembered. **Any email and any password get in**, because there is
nothing to check them against — the only thing standing between a visitor and the app is that both
boxes have something in them.

> **This screen is the visible half of something the platform deliberately deferred.** The standing
> blocker recorded on 2026-08-06 — *"There is no notion of a signed-in user, and the domain needs
> one"* — is unchanged: still no session, no user, no token, nowhere to keep one, and no backend.
> Building the screen does not un-defer that decision, and this document does not make it.

### Where the screen sits

**The login screen is the app's entry.** Owner's decision, 2026-08-10. It replaces the Kelas Online /
Kelas Offline choice as the first thing shown; that choice now sits behind it.

**There is no way back to it once it is passed.** Owner's instruction, 2026-08-10:

> "If logged in, navigate to CookingClassSelection and User unable to go back to the login screen."

So it is not a screen the user leaves and returns to — passing it puts the class selection at the
root of the app, and nothing in the app leads back.

**Every launch starts here.** Nothing is remembered between one run of the app and the next, because
nothing is stored — so a user who got in yesterday fills both boxes again today. That follows from
there being no session rather than from a decision, and it is the one obvious cost of shipping the
screen ahead of the machinery.

### What is on the screen

Top to bottom, following the design:

- **A large heading, *Login***, and beneath it one line of welcome in Bahasa Indonesia.
- **A field for an email address**, marked with an envelope, showing an example address until
  something is typed.
- **A field for a password**, marked with a padlock, whose contents are hidden as they are typed —
  **with a control on the right that reveals them**. Someone typing a password on a phone needs to
  be able to check it.
- **A way to say the password has been forgotten**, sitting under the password field at its
  right-hand end.
- **The main action, *Login***, a full-width button in the design's amber.
- **A second way in, using a Google account**, separated from the first by a divider reading
  *Atau*, and carrying the Google mark.
- **A way to get to signing up**, closing the screen: a question, then the words that lead onward.

### What each thing does when pressed

| Pressed | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Login**, both boxes filled | opens the Kelas Online / Kelas Offline choice; the login screen cannot be returned to |
| **Login**, either box empty | a message appears at the top of the screen — see below |
| **Lupa Password?** | the app's existing "not built yet" notice |
| **Lanjutkan dengan Google** | the same notice |
| **Sign up** | the same notice |

Owner's decisions, 2026-08-10. **Only *Login* leads anywhere**; the other three all answer with the
same notice the app already shows for Kelas Offline and for buying a class.

### What is checked before letting someone in

**Both boxes must have something in them. Nothing else is checked at all.** Owner's instruction,
2026-08-10:

> "The requirement for now is email and password must be filled if wanted to logged in."

That means **no rules about what was typed**: the email is not required to look like an email, the
password has no minimum length, and neither is checked against anything. Whatever is in the two
boxes is accepted.

**The button is always pressable.** It is not dimmed or disabled while the boxes are empty — pressing
it is how the user finds out, and the message is the answer.

**The owner drew the distinction explicitly**, 2026-08-10, when the agent asked how *"Field
validation → None"* sat beside the requirement that both boxes be filled:

> "What i mean validation is the validation of the textfield, but login validation is exist."

So the two are not in conflict and never were: **the fields themselves validate nothing** — no
format, no length, no message as you type — while **pressing Login does check**, and the only thing
it checks is that neither box is empty.

### The message when a box is empty

Owner's instruction, 2026-08-10:

> "If user tapped login and the textfields are empty, then show a toast at the top underneath
> dynamic island (if have) and the padding top, trailing and leading should be 8."

- **It appears at the top of the screen**, below the Dynamic Island where the device has one. On a
  device without one it sits below the status bar in the same way — [ASSUMPTION], the owner named
  only the Dynamic Island case, and the intent reads as *clear of whatever the system owns at the
  top*.
- **It is inset by 8 from the top, the leading edge and the trailing edge**, so it spans the screen
  width less that margin.
- **It appears when Login is pressed**, not while typing.
- **It disappears on its own after 3 seconds.** Owner's decision, 2026-08-10.

**It names the box that is empty.** Owner's instruction, 2026-08-10 — *"If both empty show email dan
password harus diisi, if one of them empty, just follow the previous sentence but which field is
empty"*:

| What is empty | Message |
|---|---|
| both | Email dan password harus diisi. |
| the email only | Email harus diisi. |
| the password only | Password harus diisi. |

[ASSUMPTION] the capitalisation and the full stop are the agent's; the owner supplied the words in
lower case in the middle of an English sentence.

### The message is a reusable component with three kinds

Owner's instruction, 2026-08-10: *"The Toast should be reusable, it has 3 state, error, information,
and succeed."* Whoever shows it supplies the words and the kind; it owns where it sits, how long it
stays and how it looks.

| Kind | Colour |
|---|---|
| Information | white |
| Succeed | green |
| Error | red |

Owner's decision, 2026-08-10.

**Every kind is outlined.** Owner's decision, 2026-08-10 — *"yes the toast needs outline"* — asked
because the white kind would otherwise be invisible on this screen, which is itself white. The
outline is on all three rather than on white alone, so the three read as one component in three
colours and not as two designs.

**It carries text and no icon.** Owner's decision, 2026-08-10 — *"Toast, For now only text ya"* —
asked because the colour is then the only thing distinguishing succeed from error, and green and red
are the pair red-green colour blindness collapses. The message itself still reads correctly in every
case; what a colour-blind reader loses is the tone, not the meaning. The owner has scoped this to
*for now*.

**This screen uses only the error kind.** The other two are built now because the component is meant
to serve the app, not this screen — but nothing shows them yet, so nothing exercises them.

### Light mode and portrait only

Owner's statement, 2026-08-10:

> "Currently this app forces Light Mode, no darkmode ya. And only portrait app, no landscape, FOR
> NOW. This can be changed in the future if owner wants to develop it."

**Neither is true of the app as it stands, and the agent checked before recording it.** Nothing sets
a colour scheme anywhere — no `UIUserInterfaceStyle`, no `preferredColorScheme` — so the app follows
the phone's setting today, and the existing screens only look right in dark mode because they were
built from adaptive system colours. Orientation is the Xcode default: portrait plus both landscapes
on iPhone, all four on iPad.

So the sentence above describes **what the owner intends**, not what is built. It is recorded as the
decision it is, and **making it true is work that does not exist yet** — see `## Open questions`.

It matters here because this is the first screen to be built from fixed colours rather than adaptive
ones. On a phone in dark mode it would be a white screen with text the system had turned white.

### Autofill

**The password field works with iOS password autofill.** Owner's decision, 2026-08-10, asked because
autofill offers to save a credential to iCloud Keychain — and here that credential authenticates
nothing and is checked by nothing. The owner was told and chose it: *"Password can be autofill ya."*

### Both fields keep working normally

The email field expects an email address and the password field hides what is typed. Beyond that,
nothing about typing, correcting or moving between the two fields is unusual, and nothing about it
is specified here.

## Appearance

Following the supplied design:

- **The screen is white**, its content in a single centred column with generous space above the
  heading.
- **The heading is large and black; the subtitle is smaller, muted and centred over two lines.**
- **Both fields are tall, softly rounded and filled light grey**, with their icon at the leading
  edge in the same muted grey as the placeholder text.
- **The main button is a warm golden-orange, full width, with white text**, matching the field
  corners. **Its exact value is not settled** — see `## Open questions`.
- **The Google button is the same shape and size as the main button but light grey with dark text**,
  so the two read as one action and its alternative rather than two equal choices.
- ***Atau* sits on a rule that runs to either side of it.**
- **At the foot, the question is muted and *Sign up* is dark and heavier**, so the part that can be
  pressed is the part that stands out.

**The Google mark is not available yet.** Owner's decision, 2026-08-10: *"G mark later i will add the
asset, for now just create the UI without image asset."* The button is built without it and gains it
when the owner supplies the file. This is the only screen element knowingly shipped incomplete.

### The layout the owner specified

Given on 2026-08-10 and recorded here as evidence of what was asked. **The numbers belong to the
ticket, not to this document** — they are kept because the owner supplied them directly, not because
a requirement should carry measurements.

| Element | Placement |
|---|---|
| **Login** heading | centred horizontally; vertically **0.3 above the centre of the screen** |
| Subtitle | 5 below the heading; 32 in from each side |
| Email field | 40 below the subtitle; 24 in from each side; 16 beneath it |
| Password field | the same 24 insets as the email field |
| **Lupa Password?** | the same insets as the password field; 12 above it, 24 beneath it |
| **Login** button | the same 24 insets as the password field |

[ASSUMPTION] **"0.3 from the center vertical" is read as *three tenths of the screen height above the
vertical centre***, which is where the heading sits in the supplied design. It could also have meant
three tenths down from the top; that would place it lower than the design shows.

[ASSUMPTION] ***Lupa Password?* stays aligned to the trailing edge**, as the design draws it. The
owner said its *leading* follows the password field, which the agent reads as the two sharing the
same horizontal margins rather than as a change to where the text sits.

**Everything below the Login button is left to the agent.** Owner's instruction, 2026-08-10:

> "The other below, do as you wish, since this padding can be changed after the screen fully
> presented first, let me do the padding after that."

So the divider, the Google button and the sign-up line are spaced sensibly and then adjusted by the
owner once the screen is on a device. **The numbers above are provisional for the same reason** —
they are a starting point the owner intends to revise, not a specification to defend.

> The dashed purple box at the foot of the supplied image, holding three small squares, **is not part
> of the screen.** Confirmed by the owner, 2026-08-10: *"the purple dashed box at the bottom is not
> the UI, so remove it."* It came along with the Figma export and nothing is built from it.

## Out of scope

- **Authenticating anybody.** No credentials are sent, checked or stored. Owner's instruction,
  2026-08-10.
- **Signing in with Google for real.** The button exists; the Google account flow behind it does not.
- **Creating an account**, and **recovering a password**. *Sign up* and *Lupa Password?* lead away
  from this screen; neither destination is built.
- **Any notion of a signed-in user** — no session, no user model, no token, nowhere to keep one.
  Still deferred, per the owner's decision of 2026-08-06.
- **Any backend or data-layer work.** Nothing is fetched, so there is no endpoint, no contract change
  and no library change. This is a screen and only a screen.
- **Staying logged in between launches.** Nothing is remembered, so every launch starts here.
- **Logging out.** There is nothing to leave, and nothing leads back to this screen.
- **Gating any individual feature behind being logged in.** Everything is behind the screen because
  the screen is the entry, not because anything checks.
- **Android.** Does not exist.

## Open questions

- **Locking the app to light mode and to portrait.** The owner stated both as current fact on
  2026-08-10; neither is implemented, and neither is login screen work. **It is app-wide
  configuration and needs its own piece of work** — this screen is only what made the gap visible.
- **What colour the text is on each kind of message.** Not specified; it follows from the three
  background colours once those are chosen, and the owner is choosing those after seeing the screen.
- **Whether the message behaves the same on a device with no Dynamic Island.** Assumed yes, above.
- **When real authentication is wanted.** Not part of this work, but the answer changes how much of
  the above is worth deciding now.

*Deferred by the owner rather than left open:* the **colour values**. Owner's decision, 2026-08-10 —
*"the color, for now you decide, after i tested it later, i will decide."* The agent matches them by
eye from the supplied design; the owner adjusts them once the screen is on a device, in the same way
as the spacing below the Login button.

*Resolved on 2026-08-10, before approval:* that the screen is the app's entry, that passing it cannot
be undone, that any credentials are accepted so long as both boxes are filled, that the fields
themselves validate nothing while pressing Login does, that *Lupa Password?*, *Lanjutkan dengan
Google* and *Sign up* all show the existing notice, that the empty-box message names the empty box
and clears after 3 seconds, that it is a reusable outlined component with three kinds and no icon,
that autofill is on, that the Google mark comes later, that every launch starts here, and that the
dashed box is not part of the screen. All are recorded in the body above rather than here.
